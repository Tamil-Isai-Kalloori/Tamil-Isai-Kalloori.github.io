College

Tamil Isai Kalloori — providing Tamil music education since 1944. Day college, evening college, grade examinations, online classes, distance education, and open university.

  1. Home
  2. College

Tamil Isai Kalloori (College of Tamil Music) was established on 23 January 1944 under the auspices of Chennai Tamil Isai Sangam, initially as an evening college. The day college was added in 1995.

The college is recognised by the Government of Tamil Nadu and has been affiliated to Tamil Nadu Music and Fine Arts University since 2017 for degree and diploma programmes. It also operates as a study centre for Tamil University, Thanjavur (distance education, M.Phil., and Ph.D.) and Tamil Nadu Open University.

Programmes at a Glance

  • Day College (10 AM – 4 PM): M.A. Music (Vocal), B.A. Music (Vocal), Diploma in Mridangam, Isai Asiriyar Payirchi — affiliated to Tamil Nadu Music and Fine Arts University.
  • Evening College (5 PM – 7:15 PM): Karnataka Isai Kalai, Isai Selvam, Isai Mani — in Vocal, Violin, and Bharatanatyam.
  • Grade Examinations (TISGEC): 8 levels across 9 disciplines — Vocal, Veena, Violin, Flute, Mridangam, Tavil, Nagaswaram, Bharatanatyam, Folk.
  • Online Classes: 1-year programme via Skype for students worldwide.
  • Distance Education: PG degrees, diplomas, certificates, and research programmes through Tamil University, Thanjavur.
  • Open University: General higher education through Tamil Nadu Open University.

Vocal students study ancillary instruments including Violin, Veena, Flute, Folk Music, Mridangam, and Bharatanatyam. Musicology is compulsory for all students. Scholarships are available for Nadaswaram and Tavil students. There is no age bar for admission.

Current Principal: Dr. V.V. Meenakshi.

Tamil Musical Instruments

Ancient Tamil tradition classifies musical instruments into four families, as described in the Tolkappiyam and the Silappadikaram. The grid below is an instrument taxonomy, not a course list. The college currently teaches vocal music, veena, violin, flute, mridangam, tavil, nagaswaram, Bharatanatyam, and folk forms.
