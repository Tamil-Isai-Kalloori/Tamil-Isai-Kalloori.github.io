What is a Pann?

A Pann is a melodic mode in the ancient Tamil musical system — the conceptual predecessor of the modern Carnatic raga. Panns governed the melodic framework of Tamil compositions for over two millennia, from Sangam-era poetry to the devotional hymns of the Bhakti movement.

Literary Sources

The Tolkappiyam, the earliest extant Tamil grammar, describes the Yazh (stringed instrument) associated with each of the five landscape-based thinai: Kurinji, Mullai, Marutham, Neithal, and Palai.

The Silappadikaram (c. 2nd century CE) is a treasure trove of musicological detail — its commentary references 11,991 Panns. The later commentators Adiyarkku Nallar and Arumpadhanar enriched this body of knowledge further with extensive notes on music and dance.

From the 3rd century Karaikkal Ammaiyar through the 7th–9th century Thevaram trinity (Thirugnana Sambandhar, Thirunavukkarasar, and Sundarar), Manikkavachakar’s Thiruvasagam, and the twelve Azhvars’ Divyaprabandham, thousands of compositions were set to specific Panns. The Thevaram employs 103 Panns; the Divyaprabandham references 23 Panns.

The Pann Research Conference

Since 1949, Tamil Isai Sangam has convened an annual Pann Research Conference during December 22–25 as part of the Tamil Isai Vizha. The conference invites Odhuvars (temple musicians who maintain the living Pann tradition), musicologists, Tamil scholars, and researchers to systematically study and compare the ancient Panns with modern Carnatic ragas.

The Pann–Ragam Mapping

The conference’s principal mission is to compare the 103 Panns referenced in the Pancha Marabu and Pingala Nigandu with those found in Thevaram and Divyaprabandham, and to identify the corresponding modern ragas. To date, 97 Pann-to-raga correspondences have been researched, debated, and formally announced.

All proceedings — presentations, discussions, objections, and consensus decisions — are recorded and published as conference reports, distributed free to all participants.
